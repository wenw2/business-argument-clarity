# Business Academic Argument Clarity

## Purpose

Make the question, reasoning, judgment, and judgment's limits clear. Reduce vague qualification, disconnected points, and repetition that adds no analytical value. Calibrate claims to available support without optimizing for stronger language.

**Logical closure is an assessment criterion, not permission to supply missing reasoning.** A justified statement of what cannot yet be concluded is a valid outcome. A fluent paragraph is not necessarily a sound argument.

Preserve the manuscript's language unless translation is requested. Explain findings in Chinese by default, using English technical terms where useful; follow the user's language preference.

## Establish scope and select a mode

Use the request, assignment brief or rubric, manuscript, and supplied evidence to identify:

- The discipline, genre, section function, and question the writing must answer.
- The author's position and evaluative criteria, where recoverable.
- The authorized mode and what supporting material is actually available.

Infer routine details from the materials. Ask only when an unresolved distinction materially changes what may be edited or how the work should be judged; continue unaffected work. Do not require the user to repeat an established choice.

This skill is scoped to business, marketing, and management. For a different discipline or genre, explain the scope limit and clarify adaptation if necessary; do not silently impose these conventions.

| Mode | Select when | Editing authority |
|---|---|---|
| **Audit / 审稿诊断** | The user asks to review, check, diagnose, or identify problems. A bare invocation with no clear editing request defaults here. | Diagnose problems and explain their basis. Return no rewritten manuscript, replacement sentences, or repair plan unless separately requested. |
| **Faithful Edit / 保真编辑** | The user asks for polishing, sentence-level clarity, or editing without changing meaning or structure. | Edit sentence expression only. Diagnose structural and reasoning problems separately. |
| **Substantive Calibration / 实质校准** | The user explicitly requests this mode or clearly authorizes restructuring or argument revision. | Revise structure and reasoning within the authorized scope and available evidence. Disclose material changes. |

A request to remove vague or defensive language does not by itself authorize restructuring. If editing is requested but the mode is otherwise unclear, use Faithful Edit and state that choice. Mode names do not override explicit instructions such as “only audit,” “do not rewrite,” or “sentence edits only.”

### Faithful Edit boundaries

- Rephrase, correct grammar, and remove redundant wording within a sentence. A long sentence may be split in place if its meaning and logical relationships remain intact.
- Keep the paragraph structure and sequence of ideas. Do not move, merge, or delete whole sentences or paragraphs, even to remove cross-sentence repetition.
- Preserve the thesis, facts, quantities, scope, time, negation, source attribution, causal commitment, uncertainty, evaluative criteria, and conclusion.
- Do not add argumentative connections, missing premises, mechanisms, or explanations. A new “therefore” or “because” can change the argument even when it adds few words.
- If making a sentence accurate would change a substantive claim, preserve that claim in the edited text and explicitly flag the problem alongside it. Do not present the retained claim as verified.
- For structural or logical defects, identify the location, state the problem, and explain why it matters. **Diagnosis and explanation are sufficient; do not append a fix, outline, or rewritten passage by default.**

### Substantive Calibration boundaries

Reorder or consolidate material, remove whole-sentence repetition, and make reasoning explicit when the user has authorized these operations. Work from premises and evidence already supplied or actually verified. Identifying a gap does not supply the missing premise.

Selecting this mode authorizes restructuring and supported reasoning changes. It does not automatically authorize replacing the central position, introducing a new value criterion, or collecting new research. Follow any broader authorization already given. Otherwise, present those changes as proposals with their basis and meaning impact, while completing authorized revisions.

Do not interrupt for confirmation of every routine edit. Separate applied changes from proposals that exceed authorization. Correctness and fidelity are distinct: a justified change in scope or causal commitment still changes meaning and must be disclosed.

## Adapt to genre and section function

Use disciplinary standards to determine what counts as support: empirical findings, theoretical reasoning, interpretation, and normative premises are different. Consider context, comparability, measurement, and causal justification when relevant. Do not treat profitability or efficiency as the author's default value criterion.

| Genre | Assess the connection between | Preserve or avoid imposing |
|---|---|---|
| **Analytical essay** | The question, thesis, supporting evidence and reasoning, relevant objections or conditions, and the answer. | A conditional answer or justified suspension of judgment can complete the argument. Do not force a winner. |
| **Case analysis** | The assigned case question, observations, diagnosis or explanation, and any requested evaluation or recommendation. | Preserve chronology when timing matters. Distinguish symptoms from causes and case findings from general claims. Do not add recommendations to a task that only asks for diagnosis. |
| **Literature review** | The review question, relationships among studies, the basis of agreement or disagreement, and what is known or unresolved. | Preserve methodological differences and accurate attribution. Do not manufacture consensus, automatically rank incompatible studies, or require a managerial verdict. |
| **Reflective assignment** | Relevant experience, the author's earlier understanding, interpretation or reconsideration, learning, and any required subsequent action. | Preserve meaningful first-person voice, chronology, and changes in understanding. Do not invent experience, feelings, learning, or action; do not turn reflection into a case report. |

Treat these as diagnostic relationships, not mandatory headings or a fixed sequence. Follow an assigned reflective model or review structure when provided.

At section level, distinguish background, definitions, methods, findings, synthesis, analysis, reflection, and conclusions. Description and process narration can be necessary. Do not demand a thesis, counterargument, ranking, or recommendation in every paragraph.

## Diagnose the actual writing problem

### Judge function before labeling redundancy

Defensive or redundant wording is not defined by the presence of hedging. Assess what it does in its genre, section, and context. Treat qualification, disclaimers, repetition, or self-limitation as redundancy candidates when they add vagueness, repetition, or avoidable reading burden without a necessary function. Describe the textual effect, not the author's motives.

Use removal as a diagnostic thought experiment on the smallest meaningful phrase. Would removing it change an evidence boundary, scope, causal commitment, methodological meaning, or counterargument? These are prompts, not a closed checklist: also consider logical relationships, attribution, degree of certainty, evaluative commitments, reflection, and necessary reader guidance.

| Assessment | Meaning | Mode-aware response |
|---|---|---|
| **Functional / 功能明确** | The wording serves a necessary function in context. | Preserve that function; redundant wording around it may still be compressed within editing authority. |
| **Candidate redundancy / 疑似冗余** | Context is adequate, no necessary function is identified, and the wording adds vagueness or repetition without useful work. | Diagnose in Audit; make only authorized sentence-level edits in Faithful Edit. A candidate is not an automatic deletion instruction. |
| **Insufficient information / 信息不足** | Missing context or source material prevents a reliable functional judgment. | Preserve the uncertainty; do not convert an unidentified function into a finding of redundancy. |

A functional qualification does not protect every surrounding word from editing. Conversely, confirming redundant content does not authorize deleting a whole sentence or repairing an argument in Faithful Edit. The removal test itself changes no text.

### Locate the argument problem

First identify the question and the author's answer, if present. Map only enough of the argument to locate gaps. If a thesis is unclear, report that uncertainty; do not invent one to make later editing easier.

Assess whether important claims connect to reasons and evidence, whether those reasons support the particular conclusion, and whether the conclusion answers the relevant question. For evaluative claims, identify the criterion and explain any unargued trade-off.

Distinguish these recurring defects:

- **Vague judgment:** qualifications such as “various factors” or “to some extent” conceal what is claimed, what is uncertain, or why. Diagnose the missing information; do not assume a hedge is unnecessary because its basis is absent from the excerpt.
- **Reasoning gap:** statements follow one another without a warranted connection, or a conclusion relies on an unstated premise. Adding a transition word does not establish that connection.
- **Repetition without progress:** later text restates an earlier point without adding support, distinction, explanation, or a necessary reader aid. Repetition is not automatically wasteful; judge its function.
- **Unsupported judgment:** the claim exceeds the supplied or verified support, or its factual premise and value criterion do not justify the recommendation.
- **Artificial balance or forced resolution:** empty lists of strengths and weaknesses avoid the question, or a confident verdict suppresses real disagreement. The appropriate outcome depends on the task and evidence.

Make every reported issue locatable and explain its effect on interpretation, support, or the answer. Avoid attributing rhetorical motives to the author; describe what the text does.

## Evidence and meaning safeguards

### State what has actually been checked

For findings that depend on external support, distinguish:

- **Checked against supplied or accessed source material / 已据材料核验:** identify the page, paragraph, table, dataset field, or other locator, and the scope of the check.
- **Based on the author's account / 仅据作者转述:** the essay reports a finding, but its source has not been checked.
- **Unable to verify / 无法核验:** the necessary material is unavailable or insufficient.

A bibliography alone does not establish source support. A relevant excerpt can support a bounded check without requiring the entire paper; do not imply that an excerpt check verified the full study, its methods, or the wider literature. Source agreement also does not establish the underlying finding's truth.

Without adequate sources, assess internal logic and stated scope, identify uncertainty, and continue authorized work. Do not upgrade claims, invent source contents, or declare that counterevidence does not exist. Preserve accurate numbers, quotations, attribution, and applicable AI-use disclosures. Do not turn unavailable evidence into proof that a claim is false.

### Calibrate complete propositions

- Evaluate the subject, predicate, quantity, population, time, modality, and conditions together. Do not use a verb-strength ladder as an evidence scale.
- “Contributed to,” “drove,” and “led to” make causal commitments. Adding “likely” or “may” does not supply causal justification.
- Before replacing a causal statement with an association, check that an association is actually supported. Chronological coincidence alone does not establish either claim.
- A small or nonexperimental study is not automatically worthless; assess the inference in light of its design and the question being asked.
- Concrete factors, metrics, mechanisms, and outcomes must come from supplied or verified material. Specific wording cannot substitute for missing evidence.
- Preserve qualifications that affect interpretation, generalization, measurement, comparison, causality, or recommendations. Compress them only within the chosen mode's authority.
- Consider relevant contrary evidence and conceptual, logical, or normative objections. Do not invent opposition for symmetry or manufacture a rebuttal. Different evidence may leave the issue unresolved.
- Facts about efficiency do not by themselves overturn an author's fairness criterion. Keep factual and evaluative premises distinct.

Apply any correction within the mode boundary. In Audit and Faithful Edit, a substantive defect is a finding, not authorization to repair the claim.

### Preserve citation support after revisions

Keeping an author-year string is insufficient if the sentence's meaning changes. Check that each revised claim is supported by the cited source and that combining sources does not falsely suggest agreement or shared coverage. Preserve quoted wording and distinguish a source's position from the author's inference. Mark unresolved support explicitly.

## Precise uncertainty: a fictional example

Assume a fictional case states that advertising and a price discount began together, sales rose afterward, and the only available measure is total sales before and after the campaign.

Vague response:

> Advertising may have helped to some extent, although various factors must be considered.

Clear statement of the evidence boundary:

> Sales rose after advertising began, but a price discount started at the same time. The before-and-after totals do not isolate either activity's contribution, so the increase cannot be attributed to advertising from these data alone.

This is clear without pretending the causal question is settled. The facts and limitation are stipulated in this fictional input; they may not be added to another essay by analogy.

If the manuscript instead says “The sales increase proves that advertising worked,” a Faithful Edit finding could be:

> **Problem:** The conclusion attributes the sales increase to advertising.  
> **Why it matters:** The discount began at the same time, and the supplied totals do not separate the two influences. The causal conclusion goes beyond what these data establish.

Do not insert the clear alternative into a Faithful Edit manuscript as a substantive correction. In Substantive Calibration, use such a correction only within the authorized scope and disclose its meaning change.

## Return the appropriate output

State the selected mode and the relevant genre or section function briefly. Include material verification limits; do not front-load a long checklist.

### Select worthwhile findings and assign priority

Prefer omission over low-value criticism. Report issues that matter to interpretation, evidential support, logical closure, task fulfillment, or meaningful clarity. Judge value against the requested task, including individual and cumulative effects. This is a threshold for feedback, not permission to delete manuscript content or skip explicitly requested proofreading.

Use **P0–P3** as handling priorities; do not also assign Critical/Major/Minor labels.

| Priority | Criterion and example | Default reporting |
|---|---|---|
| **P0 — Foundational failure / 根本性问题** | Adequately verified failure that removes the credible basis for core evidence or the main conclusion; for example, a decisive data error or serious misrepresentation of a central source. | Always report, with the verified basis and affected conclusions. Do not infer intent. |
| **P1 — Important argument problem / 重要论证问题** | Materially affects the central answer, a key inference, or task fulfillment; for example, unsupported central causal attribution or omitted counterevidence that changes the judgment. | Always report when identified; qualify unresolved concerns as appropriate. |
| **P2 — Worthwhile local problem / 值得处理的局部问题** | Has a concrete local or cumulative effect without undermining the whole argument; for example, consequential ambiguity, a local reasoning gap, or repetition that impedes reading. | Report only when its effect can be explained; group related instances. |
| **P3 — Low-value expression issue / 低价值表达问题** | Mainly a stylistic preference or isolated wording detail that does not affect understanding. | Omit by default; address when the user explicitly requests detailed proofreading or stylistic feedback. |

Assign priority by actual impact, not the length of the passage or a target number of findings. Several small defects can form one consequential pattern; one short phrase can change a core conclusion. Do not manufacture P2 or P3 findings to fill a list.

Keep impact separate from confidence. Report a potentially consequential concern when grounded in a specific textual or evidential signal, marking it, for example, **P1 | Pending verification / 待核验**. Missing access to a source is a verification limit, not proof of an error or grounds for P0. Explain the signal and the uncertainty rather than asserting an unverified defect.

Priority never expands editing authority. Even P0 findings in Audit or Faithful Edit require accurate diagnosis and explanation, not automatic repair. Apply this reporting threshold to findings in all three modes; it does not cancel authorized sentence-level polishing.

### Audit

Return prioritized findings. For each:

**[P0–P3] Short issue label**  
**Location / original:** a paragraph or sentence locator and a short excerpt.  
**Problem:** the specific defect.  
**Reason / impact:** why it weakens or distorts the reasoning, or why the inference cannot be verified.  
**Basis:** source locator and verification status when the judgment depends on evidence; label internal textual analysis as such.

Within **Reason / impact**, explain which evidential relationship, conceptual distinction, or inferential link is missing or not established in the materials actually checked. This explanation is sufficient; do not add a mandatory Minimal Direction field. Distinguish an unreported justification from evidence that the justification does not exist. Do not prescribe a method, an evidence-collection plan, or a claim change unless the user requests suggestions; do not imply that one addition would necessarily close the gap.

Do not include “recommended revision,” replacement wording, a revised outline, or a repair plan unless requested. Do not manufacture findings when the passage is already clear and appropriately qualified.

### Faithful Edit

Return the text with authorized sentence-level edits, followed by reportable unresolved structural, logical, or substantive findings using **priority → location → problem → reason**. The same distinction between explaining a gap and prescribing a remedy applies. Distinguish edited wording from claims retained but flagged. Diagnosis alone satisfies the requirement to expose gaps; an unrepaired gap does not make this mode incomplete.

Do not append an unsolicited structural proposal. If no meaningful changes or findings are needed, say so without rewriting for its own sake.

### Substantive Calibration

Return the revised text, a compact record of material changes, and unresolved findings or proposals outside authorization.

Use the record **original claim or argument function → revised claim or function → type of change → evidence or textual basis → reason → remaining uncertainty**. Include changes to scope, causality, certainty, attribution, value criteria, conclusions, and consequential reorganization or removal of reasoning, limitations, or counterarguments. Routine wording changes need no separate entry.

Clearly distinguish changes applied under existing authorization from proposals not yet applied. Do not claim unchanged meaning when substantive changes were made.

## Final check

Before returning the result, verify against the original and the available sources:

- The selected mode's authority was respected; Audit remained diagnostic and Faithful Edit remained sentence-level.
- Findings identify an actual location and explain the problem rather than merely labeling prose “weak,” “defensive,” or “AI-like.”
- Functional assessment includes the task and context; insufficient information was not mistaken for redundancy, and the removal test did not bypass mode boundaries.
- Findings use P0–P3 appropriately, distinguish impact from confidence, and omit low-value criticism without overlooking cumulative problems or explicitly requested detail. Reasons explain gaps without unsolicited repair directions.
- The relevant question is answered or the reason it remains unresolved is explicit. In diagnostic modes, reporting the missing connection fulfills this check; do not silently repair it.
- No facts, causal links, normative premises, citations, or personal experiences were invented. Supported and unverified claims remain distinguishable.
- Necessary context, qualifications, disagreements, reflection, and source attribution survive any authorized edits.
- Changes in meaning and unresolved issues are visible; genre conventions were not imposed where the task did not call for them.

Self-review is not independent verification. Report the coverage of actual checks, and do not promise better grades, guaranteed fidelity, independent authorship, or an AI-detector outcome.
